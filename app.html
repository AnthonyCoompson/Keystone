Programme Theory & Logic Model Reference

The Theory of Change

A logic model is a structured representation of how a programme is expected to work. It maps the causal chain from resource allocation through to systemic impact, making the underlying theory of change explicit and testable. The four-stage model — Inputs, Activities, Outputs, and Outcomes — is the foundational framework used by the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, the BC Government's Core Policy and Procedures Manual, and international bodies including the OECD Development Assistance Committee for programme evaluation.

Inputs are the resources committed before any work begins: funding envelopes, staff time, legislative authority, and partner capacity. Activities are the concrete actions taken using those inputs — engagement sessions, co-drafting processes, formal negotiations, and consultations. Outputs are the tangible, countable deliverables produced by those activities: agreements signed, committees established, frameworks published. Outcomes are the medium- to long-term systemic changes that the programme aims to achieve — shifts in jurisdiction, governance structures, or community wellbeing indicators.

The critical discipline of logic modelling is ensuring that each link in the chain is causally plausible. An activity must be substantively capable of producing its claimed outputs, and outputs must be structurally sufficient to drive the stated outcomes. When these links are weak or absent, the programme theory fails — not necessarily the programme itself, but the analyst's ability to evaluate, defend, or improve it. Keystone's audit engine is designed to surface these structural weaknesses before they become accountability gaps.

Common Logic Failures

Error TypeDescriptionHow to Fix
Dead EndAn activity with no mapped outputs or outcomes. Resources are being allocated to actions that produce no documented deliverable or systemic impact, making the activity unaccountable and unevaluable.Define at least one measurable output or outcome for every activity. If an activity genuinely produces nothing, it should be removed from the logic model.
Miracle LeapA major systemic outcome — such as jurisdictional recognition or governance transformation — that is claimed to result from activities that are structurally insufficient to produce it.Either reduce the ambition of the stated outcome to match the scale of activities, or add high-leverage activities such as legislative drafting, formal treaty negotiations, or co-governance body establishment.
Blind SpotAn output or outcome with no defined verification source or data collection mechanism. Without a named tracking method, the benchmark cannot be measured, reported on, or audited.Name a specific data collection mechanism for every output and outcome: a reporting system, a legal registry, a community survey instrument, or a government submission process.

DRIPA Alignment Notes

The Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act (DRIPA), enacted by the Province of British Columbia in 2019, requires the provincial government to take all measures necessary to ensure provincial laws are consistent with the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP). Section 7 of DRIPA specifically enables the Province and Indigenous governing bodies to enter into agreements for joint decision-making on matters affecting Indigenous peoples.

A critical evaluation consideration in DRIPA-aligned frameworks is Indigenous data sovereignty — the right of Indigenous peoples to govern the collection, ownership, and application of data about their communities, territories, and resources. The OCAP® principles (Ownership, Control, Access, and Possession), developed by the First Nations Information Governance Centre, provide the operational framework for data sovereignty in Canadian Indigenous policy contexts.

The Blind Spot audit rule is particularly relevant in DRIPA contexts. A verification source that relies solely on provincial government reporting systems — without Nation-controlled data governance — may satisfy a Treasury Board requirement while failing the substantive standard of Indigenous data sovereignty. Analysts are encouraged to name Nation-specific data stewardship bodies, community-controlled survey instruments, or jointly governed data repositories as verification sources wherever possible.
